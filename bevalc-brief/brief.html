Hemp-THC: bipartisan Senate bill would replace the 11 Dec federal ban with FDA rules; Illinois and New Jersey bans in force

T1Illinois Dept. of Revenue · NJ Division of Taxation · Drinks Intel · Axios · Chicago Sun-Times · CNN · Forbes · CSP · Marijuana MomentOct – 24 Nov

Illinois began enforcing the Illinois Hemp Act on 12 November "regardless of federal timing." Hemp products over 0.4 mg total THC per container, drinks included, can now only be sold by licensed dispensaries, and the Department of Revenue warns of seizure plus tax, penalty and interest. Binny's and other liquor stores cleared THC drinks ahead of the date, per Axios. Hemp businesses asked a court to block the law, per the Chicago Sun-Times, but no ruling has been reported. New Jersey's Division of Taxation says that from 14 November wholesalers and liquor stores may no longer sell intoxicating hemp beverages, which moves to licensed cannabis retailers. Federally, CNN reports synthetic cannabinoids lost hemp status on 12 November, and everything over 0.4 mg follows on 11 December. Marijuana Moment reports that lobbyists for Anheuser-Busch, Molson Coors and Diageo urged state attorneys general to oppose a federal delay. WSWA favors regulation over a ban. New on 16 Nov: Drinks Intel reports Total Wine & More is lobbying to keep selling THC drinks past next month's federal deadline, with its CEO arguing they belong in the three-tier system. New on 17 Nov: Shanken News Daily reports hemp THC drinks are still gaining share despite the December ban. New on 19 Nov: Sens. Tim Sheehy and Amy Klobuchar introduced the Hemp Guardrails Act, which would replace the 0.4 mg cap with FDA oversight of hemp-derived THC products, a national 21+ age limit, and bans on synthetic cannabinoids, smokable flower and imports from China, per Marijuana Moment. It has not advanced beyond introduction. New on 24 Nov: Shanken reports hemp THC brands are "bracing for the worst" with the ban 17 days out, and that the bipartisan Lawful Hemp Protection Act is also awaiting action in Congress.

Why it matters: Illinois and New Jersey accounts must now be clear of hemp-THC drinks, so distributors should be processing returns. Every other state follows on 11 December.

Moët Hennessy confirms it is leaving Empire Merchants for Southern Glazer's in metro New York; Gallo reportedly following

T3Shanken News Daily · BevNET24 Nov

Moët Hennessy confirmed to Shanken News Daily that it will leave Empire Merchants, New York's second-largest wine and spirits wholesaler, and move to Southern Glazer's in metro New York on 1 January. Gallo is reported to be moving to Southern Glazer's in the new year as well, and BevNET reports that Gallo and other major suppliers are exiting Empire. A separate Shanken report says stocks of key Moët Hennessy Champagne brands are already running short in New York ahead of the holidays.

Why it matters: This is the biggest New York distribution shake-up since Diageo's 2020 move. NYC retailers and restaurants should expect gaps in Moët, Veuve Clicquot and Hennessy supply through the holidays and new account setups with Southern Glazer's in January. Empire's future is now in question.
Shanken News Daily: Moët Hennessy · Shanken News Daily: Champagne supply

NIQ: whiskey priced $50 and up grew 4% this year while total whiskey fell 5%

T1NielsenIQ via Shanken News Daily19 Nov

In NielsenIQ channels, year to date through 3 October, whisk(e)y brands priced at $50 and over grew 4%, against a 5% decline for the total category. Shanken says US whisk(e)y is on track for a fourth straight annual volume decline.

Shanken News Daily

Kentucky bourbon production fell 29.7% in 2025 to its lowest level since 2019, and the KDA expects a further drop in 2026

T1Kentucky Distillers' Association via Drinks Intel23 Nov

Kentucky distillers filled 2.1 million barrels in 2025, down 29.7% from 3 million in 2024, according to the KDA. The figures come from inventories reported to the state Department of Revenue on 1 January. The KDA expects production to fall again in 2026.

Why it matters: Distillers are cutting output to work through high inventories as US whiskey demand softens. Expect continued pressure on bourbon pricing, and fewer new barrels in future aged releases.
Drinks Intel
