Hemp-THC: Illinois's ban is in force; New Jersey liquor stores must stop selling hemp drinks from 14 Nov; federal cutoff 11 Dec

T1Illinois Dept. of Revenue · NJ Division of Taxation · Axios · Chicago Sun-Times · CNN · Forbes · CSP · Marijuana MomentOct – 13 NovNEW SOURCE

Illinois began enforcing the Illinois Hemp Act on 12 November "regardless of federal timing." Hemp products over 0.4 mg total THC per container, drinks included, can now only be sold by licensed dispensaries, and the Department of Revenue warns of seizure plus tax, penalty and interest. Binny's and other liquor stores cleared THC drinks ahead of the date, per Axios. Hemp businesses asked a court to block the law, per the Chicago Sun-Times, but no ruling has been reported. New today: New Jersey's Division of Taxation says that from 14 November wholesalers and liquor stores may no longer sell intoxicating hemp beverages, which moves to licensed cannabis retailers. Federally, CNN reports synthetic cannabinoids lost hemp status on 12 November, and everything over 0.4 mg follows on 11 December. Marijuana Moment reports that lobbyists for Anheuser-Busch, Molson Coors and Diageo urged state attorneys general to oppose a federal delay. WSWA favors regulation over a ban.

Why it matters: Illinois and New Jersey accounts must now be clear of hemp-THC drinks, so distributors should be processing returns. Every other state follows on 11 December.

SipSource: spirits depletions stabilizing, wine still under pressure

T1SipSource (WSWA) via Shanken News Daily11 Nov

For the three months through September, spirits depletions fell 3.1% in volume and 3.5% in revenue. Wine did worse, down 6.7% in volume and 4.2% in revenue.

Why it matters: This is distributor-to-retail depletion data, so it shows what wholesalers are actually moving. Spirits declines are narrowing while wine keeps falling faster.
Shanken News Daily

Stoli returns to the US with MHW as importer and Empire Merchants and Johnson Brothers among its distributors

T3Drinks Intel6–11 Nov

Stoli Group says it is returning to the US with its full portfolio, including Stoli vodka, Kah tequila, Bayou rum and Writers' Tears, working with its longstanding distribution partners and with stock already in the country. New this week: Drinks Intel reports MHW will be importer of record, with inventory already in bonded US warehouses, and that Stoli will work with regional wholesalers including Empire Merchants in New York and Johnson Brothers, with talks under way with others. It also reports Stoli Group reached a settlement with Stoli Group USA's creditors, which clears the way for US supply to resume.

Why it matters: Distributors and retailers that dropped Stoli brands may see them offered again. Confirm availability with your distributor before resetting catalogs.

What the Democrats' midterm sweep could mean for alcohol: immigration, Canada trade and hemp

T3The Drinks Business9 Nov

Beverage stocks rose the day after the election, led by Constellation Brands, whose shares climbed as much as 7.4% per Bloomberg; Boston Beer, Brown-Forman and Molson Coors also rose. A Bank of America analyst said added immigration restrictions are less likely under a Democratic Congress, which could help Constellation's beer business. Analysts also hope for easier trade relations with Canada. The Brewers Association expects the new Congress to revisit federal rules on intoxicating hemp products.

Why it matters: These are analyst expectations, not policy. Gov affairs should watch Canada trade and the hemp definition when the new Congress sits.
